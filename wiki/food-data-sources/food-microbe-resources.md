## Overview

Microbes enter the atlas as agents of processes rather than as foods: they drive fermentation, and the food that comes out is a new form of the input food. Three resource types matter: safety inventories that say which species are used in food, metagenomic catalogues that say what is actually in fermented foods, and the fermentation literature.

## Inventories and safety lists

- **IDF Bulletin 514/2022**, the inventory of microbial food cultures with safety demonstration, is the reference list. The 2022 update extends it beyond dairy. It must be purchased.
- **EFSA's QPS list** (Qualified Presumption of Safety) is free and serves the same purpose in EU regulation.

## Metagenomic catalogues

**cFMD** (curatedFoodMetagenomicData) is the open resource, on GitHub. It covers 3,444 food metagenomes and 14,904 genomes, spanning 1,153 prokaryotic and 110 eukaryotic species-level bins. The underlying study is Carlino et al. 2024 in *Cell*, "Unexplored microbial diversity from 2,500 food metagenomes and links with the human microbiome".

## Books

*Encyclopedia of Food Microbiology*, 2nd ed. (Batt & Tortorello, 2014) and Katz's *The Art of Fermentation* (2012) cover the microbe and process side for editorial work.

## How this fits the atlas

The atlas's microbe register is Phase 4. The link it needs is simple: a microbe drives a process, and the process turns one food into another, such as cabbage into sauerkraut by lactic acid fermentation. Xanthan gum is the reminder that some foods have a microbial organism rather than a plant taxon behind them.
